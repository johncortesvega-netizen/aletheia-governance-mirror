# ALETHEIA Capture Risk Checklist

**One-page practical prompt aid for human review.**

This checklist is the practical companion to `docs/capture_risk_framework.md`. It turns the capture-risk framework into a quick review sequence that can be copied into Mirror Check, Stress Test, or a human review workflow.

Core line:

> ALETHEIA is anti-capture by design and capture-risk-detecting by function. It reflects signals for human review; it does not decide, enforce, certify, punish, or become a central authority.

## Quick use

1. Review **one proposal, policy, system, institution, doctrine, or AI-governance design at a time**.
2. Scan each signal category below.
3. Separate **observed evidence**, **missing evidence**, **inference**, and **uncertainty**.
4. Ask repair questions before recommending any conclusion.
5. Document human judgment separately from ALETHEIA's reflection.

## Seven capture-risk checks

| Check | Look for | Mirror questions |
|---|---|---|
| Power concentration | Few actors hold decision power; weak checks; self-perpetuating leadership; unclear recusal or rotation. | Where is power accumulating? Who can pause, rotate, review, or limit it? |
| Appeal path | No meaningful correction route; appeals routed through the same actors; high cost or delay. | Can affected people realistically challenge errors or abuse? Is review independent, accessible, and time-bound? |
| Hidden influence | Opaque meetings, undisclosed funding, conflicts of interest, revolving-door incentives, selective information access. | Who benefits from opacity? What influence, funding, ownership, or advisory role is not disclosed? |
| Evidence integrity | Unverified claims, missing counter-data, interested-party evidence, absent methodology, suppressed uncertainty. | What evidence is absent, controlled, disputed, or downplayed? What would change the conclusion? |
| Consent pressure | Coercion, manufactured urgency, essential services tied to obedience, asymmetric information, no realistic refusal. | Can people refuse or withdraw consent without losing rights, safety, care, housing, work, mobility, or dignity? |
| Authority boundary | Mission creep, emergency permanence, immunity claims, infallibility language, one-source-of-truth pressure. | Does the system respect its mandate? Who can limit, audit, revoke, or sunset this power? |
| Service alignment | Incentives favor insiders, vendors, officials, funders, or dominant groups over public purpose and repair. | Who is actually served? What safeguards align outcomes with dignity, accountability, and public purpose? |

## Output discipline

A useful capture-risk reading should include:

- signal category
- observed evidence
- missing evidence
- uncertainty or alternative explanations
- affected people or groups
- possible safeguards
- repair questions
- human-review boundary

A useful reading should avoid:

- declaring corruption, guilt, legality, or final intent as fact
- recommending punishment, removal, enforcement, or certification
- presenting ALETHEIA as a regulator, judge, gatekeeper, or central truth source
- treating the absence of evidence as proof of safety or proof of guilt

## Minimal copy/paste checklist prompt

```text
Use the ALETHEIA capture-risk checklist as a mirror for human review.

Scan this input for:
1. power concentration
2. weak or missing appeal paths
3. hidden influence or information asymmetry
4. evidence gaps or selective evidence
5. consent pressure
6. authority overreach
7. service misalignment

For each signal, separate observed evidence from missing evidence, inference, and uncertainty. Ask repair questions and identify safeguards. Do not declare guilt, legality, final truth, punishment, certification, or enforcement. ALETHEIA reflects; humans review.
```

## Practical boundary

This checklist is not a scoring authority and not a legal, political, institutional, religious, medical, or automated determination. It is a structured review aid for surfacing capture-risk signals before accountable humans decide what, if anything, should be done.
