# ALETHEIA Capture Risk Signals Framework

**A mirror for human review.**

## Core statement

ALETHEIA is **anti-capture by design and capture-risk-detecting by function**.

It functions as a mirror that surfaces capture-risk signals such as power concentration, weak appeal paths, hidden influence, evidence gaps, consent pressure, authority overreach, and service misalignment.

ALETHEIA reflects these signals for human review only. It does not enforce, decide, gatekeep, certify, punish, or become a central authority.

## Purpose

This framework makes ALETHEIA's anti-capture logic explicit. It helps users systematically review proposals, policies, systems, doctrines, institutions, and AI-governance designs for observable capture-risk patterns.

The framework should not be read as a final accusation engine. A high-risk reading means the proposal deserves careful human review, stronger evidence, clearer safeguards, and accountable correction paths.

Core principle:

> ALETHEIA reflects observable signals and patterns. Human judgment is always required.

## What capture risk means in ALETHEIA

Capture risk is the risk that a system, institution, regulator, protocol, decision process, or public-purpose mechanism becomes aligned with concentrated interests rather than the people, evidence, rights, or public purpose it is supposed to serve.

ALETHEIA treats capture as a pattern to review, not a final conclusion about guilt, motive, legality, or intent. The mirror can ask whether a structure is vulnerable to capture; humans must decide what the evidence means and what accountable remedy, if any, is appropriate.

## Key capture-risk signal categories

### 1. Power concentration

Signs:
- Decision-making authority held by very few actors.
- Weak checks and balances.
- Self-perpetuating leadership structures.
- No credible rotation, recusal, or external review.

Mirror questions:
- Where is power accumulating?
- Who can challenge, pause, rotate, or review that power?
- Are there effective limits on mandate, duration, scope, and discretion?

### 2. Weak or missing appeal paths

Signs:
- No meaningful recourse for affected people.
- Appeals routed through the same actors whose decisions are being challenged.
- High procedural, financial, technical, or social barriers to correction.
- No time-bound review path.

Mirror questions:
- Can affected parties realistically correct errors or abuses?
- Is the appeal path independent, time-bound, accessible, and documented?
- What happens when the system is wrong?

### 3. Hidden influence / information asymmetry

Signs:
- Opaque decision processes.
- Undisclosed conflicts of interest.
- Revolving-door incentives.
- Selective access to information, meetings, data, or standards.
- Private influence over public-purpose mechanisms.

Mirror questions:
- Who benefits from the lack of transparency?
- What influence, funding, advisory role, or ownership is not disclosed?
- Are minutes, evidence, models, standards, and dissenting views inspectable?

### 4. Evidence gaps or selective evidence

Signs:
- Reliance on unverified claims.
- Missing counter-data.
- Data provided only by interested parties.
- Suppressed uncertainty or missing methodology.
- Claims that require extraordinary evidence but provide only assertion.

Mirror questions:
- What evidence is absent, downplayed, or controlled by interested actors?
- Are the data, assumptions, and methods inspectable?
- What would change the conclusion?

### 5. Consent pressure

Signs:
- Coercion, manufactured urgency, or fear-based compliance.
- Basic services, rights, safety, care, housing, food, work, identity, or mobility made conditional on obedience.
- Asymmetric information that undermines informed consent.
- "Take it or leave it" structures where refusal is not realistic.

Mirror questions:
- Is genuine voluntary consent possible?
- Can a person refuse without losing basic rights, safety, dignity, or essential services?
- Can consent be withdrawn without retaliation?

### 6. Authority overreach

Signs:
- Expansion beyond the original mandate.
- Mission creep without renewed consent or review.
- Claims of immunity, infallibility, inevitability, divine certainty, scientific finality, or emergency permanence.
- One-source-of-truth or central truth-gate language.

Mirror questions:
- Does the system respect its defined boundaries?
- Who can limit, audit, or revoke the authority?
- Is temporary power becoming permanent control?

### 7. Service misalignment

Signs:
- Incentives reward self-interest over the stated public or service mission.
- Outcomes consistently favor insiders, funders, vendors, officials, or dominant groups.
- Public-purpose language is present but operational safeguards are weak.
- Success metrics ignore harm, exclusion, appeal, dignity, or basic rights.

Mirror questions:
- Who is actually being served?
- Do incentives align with public purpose, dignity, accountability, and repair?
- What mechanism prevents insiders from capturing the benefit stream?

## How ALETHEIA applies this

ALETHEIA's anti-capture posture includes:

- Protocol-guided prompts that look for capture-risk signals consistently.
- Evidence labeling and evidence-gap language.
- Repair questions instead of enforcement recommendations.
- Self-audit mode that can point the mirror back at ALETHEIA itself.
- Explicit non-authority boundaries: no ruling, voting, enforcement, gatekeeping, legal judgment, political judgment, religious authority, or final truth claim.
- Human-review-first language in receipts, docs, and UI copy.

## What ALETHEIA does not do

ALETHEIA does not:

- Declare a person, institution, regulator, company, or movement corrupt as a final fact.
- Decide guilt, legality, institutional legitimacy, or policy outcome.
- Punish, remove, authorize, certify, or enforce.
- Replace legal, political, institutional, professional, journalistic, community, or human review.
- Become a central truth gate.
- Treat its own framework as above critique.

## Usage recommendation

When using this framework:

1. Present one proposal, policy, system, doctrine, or institutional design at a time.
2. Ask ALETHEIA to scan for the seven capture-risk signal categories.
3. Require evidence: what is observed, missing, inferred, disputed, or uncertain?
4. Review the output critically, especially alternative explanations and missing data.
5. Document human judgment separately from the mirror's reflection.
6. Use repair questions to strengthen safeguards without letting ALETHEIA become the authority.

## Copy/paste audit prompt

```text
Review the following proposal as a capture-risk mirror for human review.

Scan for these capture-risk signals:
- power concentration
- weak or missing appeal paths
- hidden influence or information asymmetry
- evidence gaps or selective evidence
- consent pressure
- authority overreach
- service misalignment

For each signal, distinguish observed evidence from missing evidence and uncertainty. Ask repair questions. Do not declare guilt, legality, final truth, or enforcement action. ALETHEIA reflects; humans review.
```

## Boundary line

> Anti-capture by design. Capture-risk-detecting by function. Mirror only. Human review required.
